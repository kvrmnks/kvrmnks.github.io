7. Simulated Annealing and Weak Poincaré Inequalities with Inverse-Polynomial Accuracy for the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick Model
Zhe Hou, Jingcheng Liu and Yixiao Yu
preprint [arxiv]
AI Disclosure

We used AI throughout various stages of the project, both in explorations of different proof strategies and in carrying out a few heavy calculations. We started with GPT 5.5 Pro in trying to explore and understand what it takes to combine [21, 13] with [17] to get sampling algorithms in TV distance. GPT assisted with strengthening the bounds in [21, 13] in a number of ways (such as deriving 𝔼[‖m − m∗‖22] = O(1)), and it is the starting point of the current framework of analyzing the TAP residual. These bounds were enough to support the application of Girsanov comparison and the path-space correction post processing in [17] up to β < 1. Then we found out the two new advances in the SK model in [6, 18], and when GPT 5.6 Pro was released, we used it to extend our analysis to a weak Poincaré inequality throughout β < 1, as well as to the current inverse polynomial scale to support simulated annealing. We supplied high-level guidance throughout the proof developments. GPT 5.6 Pro and Ultra were also used extensively in exploring alternative proof strategies. In particular, the square-root interpolation idea in Lemma 4.2, as well as the strategy that sidesteps sub-Gaussian concentration of the TAP residual, which underlies the better concentration control in Section 4.2 and Section 4.3, were selected among a few alternatives found by GPT 5.6 Pro. GPT 5.6 Pro also claimed a false proof of an FPRAS extension based on our simulated annealing approach, which GPT 6 Astra Pro managed to fix. GPT 6 Astra Pro also found a separate deterministic FPTAS for the SK model, which we had to defer to a separate manuscript in view of OpenAI’s recent announcements [32, 35, 34, 33]. We also used Codex and GPT 5.5 xHigh, GPT 5.6 Sol Ultra, and GPT 6 Astra Ultra through various stages of preparing our manuscript, especially in checking for typos and writing. We verified all the proofs produced by AI, and we take full responsibility in their correctness.

Source: arXiv, Section 1.4.
